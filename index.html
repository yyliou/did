Preface

Difference-in-differences (DiD) is among the most widely used identification strategies in empirical economics. For decades, the two-way fixed effects (TWFE) regression served as its canonical implementation. Beginning in the late 2010s, however, a surge of methodological work revealed that TWFE can produce severely biased — and even sign-reversed — estimates when treatment adoption is staggered and effects are heterogeneous. These notes document the resulting “new DiD” literature: its theoretical foundations, its failure modes, and the modern estimators developed in response.

Structure

  • Part I — Introduction. The canonical \(2 \times 2\) setup, the potential outcomes framework and ATT, and TWFE as a multi-period generalisation. The closing section surveys the recent methodological debates that motivate the remainder of the notes.

  • Part II — Identification Assumptions. The core of DiD identification is the parallel trends assumption (PTA). Roth (2022) showed that the common practice of conditioning on a passed pre-trend test distorts the sampling distribution of post-treatment estimates, producing size distortions and reduced power. When the PTA is plausible only after conditioning on observables, Abadie (2005) proposed an IPW-DiD estimator, and Sant’Anna & Zhao (2020) extended this to a doubly robust (DR-DiD) procedure; for high-dimensional covariates, Chernozhukov et al. (2018) developed double/debiased machine learning (DML), adapted to the DiD setting by Chang (2020). When unobserved time-varying shocks threaten the PTA entirely, Arkhangelsky et al. (2021) combined synthetic control weights with DiD (SDID), and Rambachan & Roth (2023) proposed honest sensitivity analysis that bounds inference under partial violations of parallel trends. The part also covers partial compliance: Imbens & Angrist (1994) established the LATE framework, and Chaisemartin & d’Haultfœuille (2017) extended it to fuzzy DiD via the Wald-DiD estimator.

  • Part III — Staggered Adoption. Goodman-Bacon (2021) showed that under staggered treatment timing the TWFE coefficient is a variance-weighted average of all possible \(2 \times 2\) sub-comparisons — some of which carry negative weights, because already-treated units are used as implicit controls for later-treated units. This diagnosis sparked a wave of robust estimators: Callaway & Sant’Anna (2021) proposed group-time ATTs using never- or not-yet-treated units as clean controls; Borusyak et al. (2024) developed an imputation approach that fits TWFE only on untreated observations; Sun & Abraham (2021) proposed an interaction-weighted estimator for heterogeneity-robust event studies; Chaisemartin & d’Haultfœuille (2020) derived a robust estimator accommodating treatment switching; and Roth & Sant’Anna (2023) derived the semiparametrically efficient estimator for staggered rollout designs, nesting the above approaches while supporting both \(t\)-based and permutation inference.

  • Part IV — Heterogeneous Effects and Extensions. Athey & Imbens (2006) departed from the additive error structure underlying standard DiD and proposed the changes-in-changes (CiC) model, which identifies the full counterfactual distribution under non-separable unobservables. For non-negative, binary, or fractional outcomes, Wooldridge (2023) showed how pooled quasi-MLE within the linear exponential family circumvents the incidental parameters problem in staggered designs. Callaway et al. (2024) demonstrated that naive continuous-treatment TWFE suffers from analogous negative-weight problems and proposed causal dose-response functions under generalised parallel trends. The part also covers triple-differences (DDD) and quantile DiD.

Audience

These notes assume familiarity with linear regression, panel data methods, and the potential outcomes framework, at the level of a first-year graduate course in econometrics
